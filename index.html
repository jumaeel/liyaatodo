Questions, answered

Is Liyaatodo really free?

Yes — completely free. No ads, no paywall, no trial that runs out. Just open it and start.

Do I need to create an account?

No. You can start using everything instantly. Signing in with Google is optional — it just lets your tasks sync across your devices and back up to your account.

Is my data private?

Your tasks live right in your own browser until you choose to sign in. We don’t show ads and we never sell your data. If you sign in, your data is stored privately in your own account.

Does it work offline and on my phone?

Both. Liyaatodo is a Progressive Web App — add it to your home screen or desktop for a full-screen experience, and it keeps working with no connection. Changes sync the next time you’re online.

What’s the Eisenhower Matrix?

A simple, proven way to sort tasks by two questions — is it important, and is it urgent? That tells you exactly what to do first, what to schedule, what to delegate, and what to drop. Liyaatodo builds it for you automatically.

Who is it for?

Anyone who wants calmer, more focused days — students juggling deadlines, professionals running projects, or anyone tired of scattered to-do lists.

Getting started

How Liyaatodo works

A quick walkthrough of every part of the app — follow the steps below and you'll be tracking real progress in a couple of minutes.

Quick tour

tap a card to try it
1

Create & organise projects

A project is a folder for related tasks — like Studies, Work, or Errands. Use the “New project…” box at the bottom of the left sidebar, type a name, and hit the + button. Hover a project to rename ✏️ it, reorder ▲▼ it, or delete it. Click its name to open it — or click the Liyaatodo logo any time to jump back to the Dashboard. The ☰ button in the top bar hides or shows the sidebar.

2

Add, label & edit tasks

Open a project and use the Add task form. Give it a title, a priority (High / Medium / Low), a deadline (defaults to one week out), an estimated time (30m, 2h, 1h30m…), and an optional label (like study or errand) to group similar tasks — click any label chip to filter by it. Tag a task ★ Important and ⚡ Urgent to power the matrix. Use Sort and the List / Matrix toggle, plus the search box, to find things fast. Hit the ✏️ pencil to edit any detail later, and tick the checkbox when it’s done.

3

Pick your “Today’s Focus”

Open Today’s Focus and choose up to 5 tasks you’ll finish today — pulled from any project (search to find them fast). Ticking one off here also completes it in its project. You get a live clock, a “time left today” bar, a custom day countdown (to an exam, trip or Eid), a fresh quote every few minutes, and a ⛶ Fullscreen focus mode. Each new day the list resets so you start fresh — finished tasks stay done in their projects.

4

See what’s coming up

The What’s coming up screen gathers every task that has a deadline from all your projects and lists them soonest first, grouped into Overdue, Due today, Tomorrow, Next 7 days and Later. Tick one to complete it, hit the ✏️ pencil to edit, or tap a row to jump to its project. The sidebar shows a red badge when anything’s overdue.

5

Watch your progress

The Dashboard is your command centre: total / completed / active / overdue counts, an overall completion gauge, a bar for every project (with its total estimated time), and a priority breakdown. The Focus Matrix here spells out exactly what’s important & urgent for you and lists those tasks — tap one to jump straight to its project. Everything updates live as you check tasks off.

6

Prioritise with the Matrix

Inside a project, switch from List to Matrix view to see the Eisenhower Matrix: four boxes — Do (important & urgent), Schedule, Delegate, and Delete. Drag a task into a box to set its importance/urgency. It’s the fastest way to decide what actually deserves your attention.

7

Make it yours

In Settings you can switch theme — warm Ink & Amber or cool Periwinkle — and the 🌙 / ☀️ button in the top bar flips dark / light. Add your own motivation quotes and manage the whole library, back up everything to a file (and import it back), reset the app cache, or erase data by scope. The 🔔 bell gives overdue and due-soon reminders (with optional browser notifications), and you can install Liyaatodo as an app for an offline, full-screen experience. The 🎁 What’s new button shows the latest changes.

8

Sign in to save & sync

Tap your avatar (top-right) → Account & sync → Continue with Google. Your Google profile picture appears in the top bar, everything saves to your account automatically, and it syncs across all your devices — finish a task on your phone and it’s updated on your laptop. Signed out, your data still saves in this browser.

Understanding the Eisenhower Matrix

the heart of prioritising

Named after US President Dwight D. Eisenhower, this method sorts every task by two simple questions: Is it important? (does it move you toward your real goals?) and Is it urgent? (does it need attention right now?). Crossing those two answers gives four quadrants — and each one tells you exactly what to do. In Liyaatodo you set a task's ★ Important and ⚡ Urgent flags (or just drag it into a box in the project's Matrix view), and it lands in the right quadrant automatically.

1 · Do Important · Urgent

Do these first, today. Crises and real deadlines — an assignment due tonight, a bill, something on fire. If it matters and the clock is ticking, it belongs here. Don't let this pile grow.

e.g. “Submit the report due tonight”, “Fix the broken booking page”.

2 · Schedule Important · Not urgent

Plan a time for these — this is where you grow. No deadline pressure yet, but they build your future: studying, skills, health, relationships. Give them a deadline so they don't slip into quadrant 1 as emergencies.

e.g. “Revise for next month's exam”, “Learn a new skill”, “Exercise”.

3 · Delegate Urgent · Not important

Delegate it, or knock it out fast. It feels pressing but doesn't really move your goals — interruptions, some emails and calls, busywork. Hand it off if you can, or batch it quickly so it doesn't eat your day.

e.g. “Reply to a routine email”, “Forward a request to the right person”.

4 · Delete Not important · Not urgent

Drop or defer these. Distractions and time-wasters that neither matter nor are urgent. Be honest — deleting them is what frees your time for quadrants 1 and 2.

e.g. “Mindless scrolling”, “Tasks that no longer serve a goal”.

The goal: spend most of your time in quadrant 2 (Schedule). People who plan important work before it becomes urgent stay calm and in control; people who live in quadrant 1 are always firefighting. Liyaatodo's deadlines, reminders and the dashboard matrix are built to keep you ahead.
